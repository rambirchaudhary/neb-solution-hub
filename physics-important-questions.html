Fluid Mechanics Questions: 7

  1. 1

    Define surface tension and state whether it depends on the surface area of a liquid. Show that the surface energy per unit area of a liquid is numerically equal to its surface tension. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Surface tension is the force per unit length acting along the surface that tends to minimize surface area. It is independent of total surface area; surface energy per unit area equals surface tension because work done to increase area by dA is γ dA.

  2. 2

    State Pascal's law and explain why fluid pressure does not depend on the cross-sectional area of its container. For a cylindrical vessel, determine the height to which it must be filled with liquid so that the force exerted on its curved side equals the force exerted on its base. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Pascal's law: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished. Pressure at depth depends on height, not area. (Derivation and algebraic solution omitted here for brevity.)

  3. 3

    State Pascal's law and derive P = ρgh. A uniform tube has two vertical arms joined by a horizontal section 8 cm long at the bottom. Water filling 22 cm of one arm is poured in, and oil of specific gravity 0.8 filling 22 cm is poured into the other arm. Determine the position of the interface between the two liquids. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Pressure at depth: P = ρgh. For the interface, equate pressures at the same horizontal level; solve for interface height using densities ρ_water and ρ_oil (ρ_oil = 0.8 ρ_water).

  4. 4

    State Archimedes' principle and discuss whether it applies to floating bodies. An alloy made of copper (density 8.9 g/cm³) and zinc (density 7.1 g/cm³) weighs 12.9 g in air and 11.3 g when fully submerged in water. Calculate the mass fraction of each metal in the alloy. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Archimedes' principle: buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid. Use apparent weight loss to compute displaced water mass and solve simultaneous equations for mass fractions of copper and zinc.

  5. 5

    State the law of floatation and comment on whether a floating body is in equilibrium. A wooden cube, loaded with a 200 g mass, just floats in water. When the mass is removed, the cube rises by 2 cm. Find the dimensions of the cube. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Law of floatation: weight of floating body equals weight of displaced fluid. Use change in submerged depth to compute cube volume and side length.

  6. 6

    Define streamline flow and explain the conditions under which it becomes turbulent. An aircraft wing is to be designed to produce a lift of 1000 N per square metre of wing area. If air moves at 150 m/s beneath the wing, find the required air speed above the wing. (Take air density = 1.3 kg/m³) [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Streamline (laminar) flow: fluid particles follow smooth paths; turbulence occurs at high Reynolds number. Use Bernoulli's principle to relate pressures and velocities to find required speed above wing.

  7. 7

    Define the viscosity of a fluid and describe how it varies with temperature. An air bubble of radius 1 cm rises at a constant velocity of 3.5 mm/s through a liquid of density 1.75 × 10³ kg/m³. Calculate the coefficient of viscosity of the liquid, ignoring the density of air. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Viscosity measures internal friction; typically decreases with temperature for liquids. Use Stokes' law for terminal velocity of a sphere to compute viscosity: η = (2/9) (ρ_l - ρ_g) g r² / v (approx.).
