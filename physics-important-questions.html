Physics — Important Questions

NEB Grade 12 — chapter-wise important questions for quick revision and exam practice.

Important Questions content

Fluid Mechanics Questions: 7

  1. 1

    Define surface tension and state whether it depends on the surface area of a liquid. Show that the surface energy per unit area of a liquid is numerically equal to its surface tension. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Surface tension is the force per unit length acting along the surface that tends to minimize surface area. It is independent of total surface area; surface energy per unit area equals surface tension because work done to increase area by dA is γ dA.

  2. 2

    State Pascal's law and explain why fluid pressure does not depend on the cross-sectional area of its container. For a cylindrical vessel, determine the height to which it must be filled with liquid so that the force exerted on its curved side equals the force exerted on its base. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Pascal's law: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished. Pressure at depth depends on height, not area. (Derivation and algebraic solution omitted here for brevity — include step-by-step in teacher notes.)

  3. 3

    State Pascal's law and derive the expression P = ρgh for fluid pressure at depth. A uniform tube has two vertical arms joined by a horizontal section 8 cm long at the bottom. Water filling 22 cm of one arm is poured in, and oil of specific gravity 0.8 filling 22 cm is poured into the other arm. Determine the position of the interface between the two liquids. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Pressure at depth: P = ρgh. For the interface, equate pressures at the same horizontal level; solve for interface height using densities ρ_water and ρ_oil (ρ_oil = 0.8 ρ_water).

  4. 4

    State Archimedes' principle and discuss whether it applies to floating bodies. An alloy made of copper (density 8.9 g/cm³) and zinc (density 7.1 g/cm³) weighs 12.9 g in air and 11.3 g when fully submerged in water. Calculate the mass fraction of each metal in the alloy. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Archimedes' principle: buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid. Use apparent weight loss to compute displaced water mass and solve simultaneous equations for mass fractions of copper and zinc.

  5. 5

    State the law of floatation and comment on whether a floating body is in equilibrium. A wooden cube, loaded with a 200 g mass, just floats in water. When the mass is removed, the cube rises by 2 cm. Find the dimensions of the cube. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Law of floatation: weight of floating body equals weight of displaced fluid. Use change in submerged depth to compute cube volume and side length.

  6. 6

    Define streamline flow and explain the conditions under which it becomes turbulent. An aircraft wing is to be designed to produce a lift of 1000 N per square metre of wing area. If air moves at 150 m/s beneath the wing, find the required air speed above the wing. (Take air density = 1.3 kg/m³) [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Streamline (laminar) flow: fluid particles follow smooth paths; turbulence occurs at high Reynolds number. Use Bernoulli's principle to relate pressures and velocities to find required speed above wing.

  7. 7

    Define the viscosity of a fluid and describe how it varies with temperature. An air bubble of radius 1 cm rises at a constant velocity of 3.5 mm/s through a liquid of density 1.75 × 10³ kg/m³. Calculate the coefficient of viscosity of the liquid, ignoring the density of air. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Viscosity measures internal friction; typically decreases with temperature for liquids. Use Stokes' law for terminal velocity of a sphere to compute viscosity: η = (2/9) (ρ_l - ρ_g) g r² / v (approx.).

Heat & Thermodynamics Questions: 12

  1. 1

    Explain the term thermodynamic variable and describe what makes up the internal energy of a system. Water falls from a height of 350 m; assuming all the energy of the fall converts into heat, calculate the rise in temperature. (Specific heat capacity of water = 4200 J/kg·K) [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Thermodynamic variables: state variables like P, V, T, U. Internal energy includes kinetic and potential energies of particles. Use mgh = mcΔT to compute ΔT.

  2. 2

    Define the molar heat capacity of a gas and explain why its value at constant pressure exceeds that at constant volume. Raising the temperature of 3 moles of an ideal gas from 25℃ to 30℃ at constant pressure requires 105 calories. Find the heat needed to produce the same temperature rise at constant volume. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Cp > Cv because at constant pressure work is done by expansion. Use Cp - Cv = R and energy relations to compute heat at constant volume.

  3. 3

    Explain what is meant by an isothermal process and derive the expression for the work done when a gas expands from pressure P1 to P2 at constant temperature. 5 moles of gas at 1 atm and 20℃ expand isothermally from 5 litres to 50 litres; calculate the work done by the gas. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Isothermal: constant temperature; work W = nRT ln(V2/V1). Substitute values to compute W.

  4. 4

    Explain the significance of the first law of thermodynamics. At constant volume, the heat supplied equals the change in internal energy, but this relation does not hold at constant pressure - explain why. Two moles of an ideal gas are heated from 250 K to 500 K at constant pressure; sketch the P-V diagram for this process and find the work done. [1+2+1+1]

    Answer (concise): First law: ΔU = Q − W. At constant pressure, part of heat does work (PdV). Work = ∫PdV; for ideal gas at constant pressure W = PΔV = nRΔT.

  5. 5

    Discuss the importance of the P-V diagram in thermodynamics. For the process shown involving 0.45 mol of an ideal gas, determine the temperatures at the two marked points, the work done during the process, and the heat required to raise the internal energy by 15000 J. [2+3]

    Answer (concise): P-V diagrams visualize processes and work. Use ideal gas law and area under curve for work; ΔU = nCvΔT to relate heat and internal energy change.

  6. 6

    Explain the meaning of an adiabatic process. Starting from PV^γ = constant, derive the expression for work done during an adiabatic process, W = (P1V1 − P2V2)/(γ − 1). During the adiabatic expansion of 0.5 mol of argon gas, the temperature drops from 520 K to 70 K; find the work done.

    Answer (concise): Adiabatic: no heat exchange. Derive W using PV^γ relation and ideal gas law; compute W using ΔU = nCvΔT and W = −ΔU for adiabatic reversible processes.

  7. 7

    State Kelvin's statement of the second law of thermodynamics, and give the efficiency expression for a Carnot engine running between two temperatures. A heat engine draws 500 calories from a source at 200℃ and rejects 200 calories to a sink, giving it 75% efficiency. Find the sink temperature, and determine how much the sink temperature must be lowered to raise the efficiency to 85%. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Kelvin statement: impossible to convert heat fully into work without other effects. Carnot efficiency η = 1 − Tc/Th (in Kelvin). Use energy flows and efficiency relations to compute temperatures.

  8. 8

    Describe a petrol engine and explain why it requires a spark plug. Sketch the P-V diagram for the Otto cycle, and state how its efficiency relates to the compression ratio. [1+1+2+1]

    Answer (concise): Petrol engines use spark ignition; Otto cycle efficiency increases with compression ratio: η = 1 − 1/r^(γ−1).

  9. 9

    Identify which type of engine the accompanying P-V diagram represents and justify your answer. Explain what the marked straight line on the diagram signifies, and discuss whether this engine type needs a spark plug. [2+2+1]

    Answer (concise): (Interpretation depends on diagram.) Provide reasoning based on isochoric/isobaric/adiabatic segments and ignition method.

  10. 10

    Define the coefficient of performance of a refrigerator, and explain why external work input is necessary to run one. A refrigerator absorbs 200 J of heat while 100 J of work is supplied to it; calculate its coefficient of performance. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): COP = Qc / W. Here COP = 200 / 100 = 2.

  11. 11

    Explain the concept of entropy and show how it arises from the first law of thermodynamics. Calculate the change in entropy when 1 kg of ice melts at 0℃. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Entropy relates heat transfer to reversible temperature: ΔS = ∫dQ_rev/T. For melting, ΔS = Q/T = mL_f / T (use latent heat of fusion).

  12. 12

    Explain why the expansion stroke of an engine is referred to as the power stroke, and why diesel engines are built with a higher compression ratio. A petrol engine burns 5 kg of fuel per hour and delivers 20 kW of power; given the calorific value of petrol is 11 × 10³ kcal/kg, find the engine's efficiency. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Power stroke produces useful work. Diesel higher compression for auto-ignition. Compute energy input per second and efficiency = P_out / P_in.

Magnetic Effect of Current Questions: 9

  1. 1

    Given that the magnetic force on a charged particle is F = q(v × B), show that a proton moving perpendicular to a magnetic field follows a circular path. Explain under what condition this proton could experience zero force. Find the force on a 5 m wire carrying a 2 A current in a 2 T magnetic field, given that the wire's cross-sectional area vector is parallel to the current. [2+2+1]

    Answer (concise): For v ⟂ B, magnetic force provides centripetal force: qvB = mv²/r. Zero force when v ∥ B. Force on wire: F = I L × B; compute magnitude ILB.

  2. 2

    Explain the right-hand thumb rule used in magnetism. Derive the expression F = q(v × B) for the magnetic force on a moving charge, and use it to define what constitutes a magnetic field of 1 tesla at a point. [1+3+1]

    Answer (concise): Thumb rule gives direction of magnetic force. 1 T defined by force of 1 N on 1 C moving at 1 m/s perpendicular to field.

Electromagnetic Induction Questions: 9

  1. 1

    State Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction. A bicycle spoke rotates within a magnetic field, oriented perpendicular to the field — determine the induced emf this produces. A plane with a 40 m wingspan flies due north at 500 m/s through the Earth's vertical magnetic field component of 5 × 10⁻⁴ T; find the voltage induced across its wings. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): Faraday: emf = −dΦ/dt. For moving conductor emf ≈ Bℓv (for perpendicular motion). Use ℓ = 40 m, v = 500 m/s, B = 5×10⁻⁴ T to compute emf.

Alternating Current (AC) Questions: 7

  1. 1

    Define a phasor and explain how it is used to represent alternating current. Explain why the rms value of AC is more practically meaningful in everyday use than its mean value.

    Answer (concise): Phasor: complex representation of sinusoidal quantities; RMS relates to equivalent DC heating effect.

Wave Optics Questions: 8

  1. 1

    Explain the concept of a wavefront and describe how a plane wavefront can be produced. Using the wave theory of light, derive the law of refraction. [1+1+3]

    Answer (concise): Wavefront: locus of points in phase. Use Huygens' principle to derive Snell's law.

Radioactivity Questions: 4

  1. 1

    Explain how a certain particle behaves like an electron without actually being one. In a radioactive decay experiment, three emitted particles A, B, and C were observed in an electric or magnetic field. Identify particles A, B, and C with reasoning, and explain why particle C showed no deflection. [1+1+2+1]

    Answer (concise): Identify α (positive), β (negative), γ (neutral) based on deflection; γ shows no deflection due to no charge.

Recent Trends Questions: 2

  1. 1

    Define seismic waves and explain how P-waves and S-waves transmit energy from the hypocenter to the epicenter. Discuss why Love waves tend to be more destructive. [1+2+2]

    Answer (concise): P-waves compressional, S-waves shear; Love waves cause large horizontal motion and are destructive near surface.

  2. 2

    Define nanotechnology and discuss its advantages over conventional materials technology, along with its potential future applications.

    Answer (concise): Nanotech manipulates matter at nanoscale; advantages include novel properties, high surface area, and tailored functionality for medicine, electronics, energy.
